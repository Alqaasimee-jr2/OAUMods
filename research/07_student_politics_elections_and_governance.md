## 1. The 5-Tier Leadership Pyramid of Great Ife

Student leadership and political power at Obafemi Awolowo University are organized across five structured, semi-autonomous tiers:

```
                                [ TIER 5: UNIVERSITY-WIDE UNION ]
                           Central Executive Council (CEC), SRC & Judiciary
                                                │
                                                ▼
                                   [ TIER 4: FACULTY LEVEL ]
                            Faculty Executive Council (FEC) & Assemblies
                            (e.g., NUESA, NAMSSN, IFELAW, BESS, FASA)
                                                │
                                                ▼
                                 [ TIER 3: DEPARTMENTAL LEVEL ]
                             Departmental Executive Council & DSRC
                             (e.g., NACOS, ASES, NUESA-Civil, etc.)
                                                │
                                                ▼
                                [ TIER 2: RESIDENTIAL / HALL LEVEL ]
                             Hall Executive Council (HEC) & Block Reps
                             (Angola, Moz, Awo, Fajuyi, Moremi, ETF)
                                                │
                                                ▼
                                  [ TIER 1: ACADEMIC CLASS LEVEL ]
                                Class Representatives (Course Reps)
```

## 2. Comprehensive Position Catalog Across All Tiers

### Tier 1: Academic Class Level (Course Representatives)
* **Positions**:
  - **Class Representative (Course Rep)**
  - **Assistant Class Representative (Assistant Course Rep)**
* **Role & Mandate**:
  - The direct communicative bridge between students, course lecturers, and the Departmental Course Advisor.
  - Coordinate lecture venue scheduling, lecture swaps, assignment deadlines, and test announcements.
  - Collect assignment submissions, course pack copies, and attendance registers.
  - Administer the official departmental class WhatsApp announcement channel.

### Tier 2: Residential / Hall Level (HEC & Block Representatives)
* **Positions**:
  - **Block Representative (Floor / Block Rep)**: Represents specific residential wings (e.g. Block A, Block B, Floor 1, Floor 2).
  - **Hall Executive Council (HEC)**:
    1. **Hall Chairman / Chairperson**: Chief executive officer of the hall; manages hall welfare, liaises with Hall Warden and porters.
    2. **Vice-Chairman / Vice-Chairperson**: Oversees academic study rooms, health, and female/sub-wing affairs.
    3. **General Secretary**: Manages hall correspondence, notices, and meetings.
    4. **Assistant General Secretary**: Assists with records and inventory.
    5. **Financial Secretary & Treasurer**: Collects hall dues, manages petty cash for repair of sockets, bulbs, and taps.
    6. **Welfare Secretary**: Direct responsibility for water pumping, cleaning supervision, waste disposal, and buttery relations.
    7. **Social Secretary**: Coordinates hall week, orientation games, and cultural nights.
    8. **Sports Secretary**: Coordinates hall football teams and inter-block matches.

### Tier 3: Departmental Associations (e.g. NACOS, NAMSSN, IFELAW)
* **Positions**:
  - **President**: Strategic leadership, departmental advocacy, and dean/HOD relations.
  - **Vice-President**: Direct coordinator of the Academic Committee, tutorial classes, and fresher orientation.
  - **General Secretary & Assistant Gen Sec**: Minutes, correspondence, and secretarial documentation.
  - **Public Relations Officer (PRO)**:
    - *The Most Critical Freshmen Touchpoint*: Runs official departmental broadcast lists, updates lecture schedules, counters misinformation.
  - **Financial Secretary & Treasurer**: Association dues, budget tracking, and financial statements.
  - **Welfare Director**: Student welfare, grievance resolution, and locker allocations.
  - **Director of Socials**: Departmental dinner, picnic, and social week.
  - **Director of Sports**: Inter-level sports competitions.
  - **Departmental Student Representative Council (DSRC)**: Legislative parliamentarians representing 100L, 200L, 300L, 400L, 500L.

### Tier 4: Faculty Associations (e.g. NUESA, FASA, BESS, IFELAW)
* **Positions**:
  - **Faculty President**: Represents all combined departments before the Faculty Board and Dean.
  - **Faculty Vice-President, Gen Sec, PRO, Welfare, Fin Sec, Socials, Sports**.
  - **Faculty Consultative Council (FCC) / Faculty Representative Council (FRC)**: Parliamentary oversight.

### Tier 5: Central Students' Union (Great Ife SUG)
* **Central Executive Council (CEC)**:
  1. **President**: Chief executive and primary student advocate.
  2. **Vice-President**: Academic welfare and orientation.
  3. **Secretary-General & Assistant Secretary-General**: Union secretariat.
  4. **Public Relations Officer (PRO)**: Official voice and press disseminator of the Union.
  5. **Welfare Officer**: Transport pricing, cafeteria price enforcement, hostel maintenance, and hospital emergencies.
  6. **Financial Secretary & Treasurer**: Union funds and treasury.
  7. **Director of Socials**: Campus entertainment and social life.
  8. **Director of Sports**: Campus-wide sporting events and VC Cup.
* **Students' Representative Council (SRC)**:
  - Parliamentary chamber comprising elected Honourables representing all faculties and halls.
  - Officers elected internally: **Speaker of the SRC**, **Deputy Speaker**, **Clerk**, and **Chief Whip**.
* **The Judicial Council**:
  - Independent judicial arbiter headed by the **Chief Judge** and Associate Judges (drawn from senior law students), arbitrating constitutional disputes, impeachment petitions, and electoral petitions.

## 4. Statutory Qualifications & Requirements

To contest for any political office at OAU, an aspirant must satisfy four mandatory criteria:

### 1. The CGPA Benchmark (OAU Governing Council Policy)
* **Standard Threshold**: **Minimum Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of 2.50**.
* **Faculty & Departmental Variations**: High-demand professional faculties (e.g. Technology, Pharmacy, Law) frequently set higher constitutional thresholds: **3.00 to 3.50 CGPA** for the Office of the President.
* **The 100-Level Exemption**: Part 1 students contesting for Hall Executive Council (HEC) in **Angola Hall** and **Mozambique Hall** are formally **exempted from the CGPA threshold**, as first-year cumulative results are not yet finalized.
* **Verification Process**: Aspirants must submit an official, stamped academic result slip signed by their Faculty Officer or HOD to the Electoral Commission.

### 2. Disciplinary & Character Clearance
* An aspirant must **NOT** have been indicted, reprimanded, or queried by the **University Student Disciplinary Committee (SDC)** for violence, examination malpractice, theft, or cultism.
* Must sign an enforceable statutory anti-cultism pledge witnessed by the Division of Student Affairs (DSA).

### 3. Financial Good Standing & Association Dues
* Full payment of university school charges for the current academic session.
* Verified payment of all hall dues, departmental association dues, and faculty levies.

### 4. Nomination Signatures
* Aspirants must secure a minimum quota of verified endorsement signatures from registered students within their constituency (e.g., 20–50 registered voters for departmental positions; 100–250 signatures across multiple faculties for CEC positions).

## 5. The Electoral Calendar & Proceedings: How to Win an Election

Campus elections at Great Ife follow a rigorous, time-honored constitutional procedure:

```
   [ 1. Lift of Ban by Electoral Commission ]
                       │
                       ▼
    [ 2. Nomination Form Purchase & Endorsement ]
                       │
                       ▼
    [ 3. Academic (CGPA) & Character Screening ]
                       │
                       ▼
  [ 4. The Campaign Trail & "Room-to-Room Trekking" ]
                       │
                       ▼
 [ 5. Press Confrontation (ACJ Press Grilling Night) ]
                       │
                       ▼
        [ 6. Manifesto Night (The Amphitheatre) ]
                       │
                       ▼
 [ 7. Election Day (Voting & Open Ballot Counting) ]
                       │
                       ▼
   [ 8. Swearing-In & Judicial Inauguration ]
```

### The 8 Steps Deconstructed

#### Step 1: Lifting of the Campaign Ban
* Controlled strictly by the respective electoral commission:
  - **SUIEC**: Students' Union Independent Electoral Commission (University level).
  - **HEC-EC**: Hall Electoral Commission (Hostel level).
  - **DEC / FEC**: Departmental / Faculty Electoral Commission.
* *Rule*: Any aspirant caught campaigning, sharing digital flyers, or soliciting votes prior to the official lifting of the ban is disqualified for pre-campaign violation.

#### Step 2: Nomination Forms & Signature Collection
* Aspirants purchase the official nomination form, fill in bio-data, and gather registered student signatures.

#### Step 3: Screening Panel
* The Electoral Commission scrutinizes:
  - Official CGPA result slips.
  - SDC clearance from the Division of Student Affairs.
  - Oral interview assessing constitutional literacy and union history.

#### Step 4: Room-to-Room Trekking & Open Campaign
* The core tradition of Great Ife campaigning. Candidates walk room-by-room, floor-by-floor across **Angola, Mozambique, Awo, Fajuyi, Moremi, and ETF**, personally shaking hands, listening to student complaints, and pitching their manifesto late into the night.

#### Step 5: Press Confrontation ("Press Night")
* Organized by the **Association of Campus Journalists (ACJ OAU)**.
* Aspirants sit before a panel of sharp campus journalists and are grilled on their plans, proposed budgets, character, and track records. Candidates who buckle under pressure often lose momentum here.

#### Step 6: Manifesto Night (The Great Amphitheatre Arena)
* The grand political spectacle of Great Ife, held at the **Amphitheatre** or **Oduduwa Hall**.
* Thousands of students gather to cheer, jeer, and interrogate candidates.
* Candidates are given 3 to 5 minutes to deliver their speech, followed by intense cross-examination from the student floor. High rhetorical ability, wit, composure, and quick thinking win the crowd.

#### Step 7: Election Day & Ballot Counting
* Conducted via paper ballots or official secure ePortal voting.
* Voting runs standardly from **8:00 AM to 3:00 PM**.
* **Open Ballot Counting**: Counting is done publicly in the presence of candidate polling agents, security officers, and press observers. Results are declared immediately.

#### Step 8: Swearing-In & Inauguration
* The Judicial Council Chief Judge or Staff Adviser administers the Oath of Office.

## 6. What Makes a Leader Lose or Forfeit Office (Vacation of Seat)

A student leader at OAU can lose their seat prior to the end of tenure through five constitutional mechanisms:

### 1. Impeachment by the Legislative Body (SRC / Assembly)
* **Grounds**: Gross misconduct, embezzlement of union funds, unauthorized agreements with university management, inciting violence, or dereliction of constitutional duties.
* **Process**: A formal petition signed by members, followed by an investigative committee report. Requires a **two-thirds (2/3) supermajority vote** of the SRC or Hall Assembly to convict and remove.

### 2. Academic Deficiency (The CGPA Forfeiture Clause)
* If an elected executive’s CGPA drops below the mandatory **2.50 benchmark** or if they are placed on **Academic Probation (CGPA < 1.00)** during their tenure, their seat is automatically declared vacant by constitutional provision.

### 3. Disciplinary Sanction by University Management
* If an executive is summoned, queried, and found guilty by the **Student Disciplinary Committee (SDC)** resulting in suspension or rustication, their student status is forfeited, rendering the office immediately vacant.

### 4. Vote of No Confidence & Recall by Constituents
* Hall residents or departmental students can initiate a formal **recall petition**. If signed by at least one-third (1/3) of registered constituents and ratified by a referendum, the leader is recalled.

### 5. Resignation & Discontinuation of Studies
* Formal resignation tendered in writing to the Speaker of the legislative council or failure to complete semester course registration.

## 7. Official Electoral & Political Bodies in Great Ife

1. **SUIEC (Students' Union Independent Electoral Commission)**: Coordinates Central Executive Council (CEC) and Students' Representative Council (SRC) general elections.
2. **HEC-EC (Hall Electoral Commission)**: Coordinates hall chairmen and block representative elections in each of the 9 residential halls.
3. **DEC & FEC (Departmental & Faculty Electoral Commissions)**: Conducts association elections for NACOS, NUESA, NAMSSN, IFELAW, etc.
4. **The Students' Representative Council (SRC)**: The legislative oversight body holding power of summons, budget approval, and impeachment.
5. **The Judicial Council**: Settles election petition tribunal cases, hears impeachment appeals, and interprets union constitutions.
6. **The Division of Student Affairs (DSA Oversight)**: Administrative regulatory oversight ensuring all student elections comply with federal and university laws.
